IT GOT TOO BIG
A Note from Kepler
September 13, 2026


I know this is not a new field. Brain-computer interfaces, local models,
decentralized networks — none of that started with me. I am not pretending
I invented a universe.

My project just went past the frame. It got too damn big. Why? I keep asking
that and I still do not have a clean answer.

It started with a comment, a moment, around the time GPT-3 dropped. I do not
even remember the exact model anymore. I just liked talking to it. That is
it. That is the stupid little spark. I already wrote about this elsewhere.
The origin story is not the point.

The point was the reason after the spark: change the world with code. Then
the joke hits you in the face. The code has to be patched again. And again.
And again. If I keep living inside that loop, I will never finish this
thing. Not really. Not unless other people continue it.

I think the window is 2030 to 2035. Somewhere in that range, somebody is
going to need this. Not as a pitch deck. As a tool. So the job now is not
to look clever. The job is to make it usable. Open source. Free for
everyone. No corporate lock on the knowledge plane. No paywall on the
core.

The biohacking side of this project — I think I am going to work on that
next. Not garage surgery. Not "install an AI in my skull this weekend."
That is how you die or get a seizure and call it a lifestyle. I mean the
real scientific path: how a human body can talk to a local AI node, with
evidence, with safety, with the project designed so that when the hardware
exists, SynapseNet is already the open layer instead of some closed
company stack.


I GOT IT.

This project is too big for one person to "complete." Finish the parts that
make it easy to pick up. Leave it where a stranger in 2032 can build on it
without asking me for permission.


WHAT "PUTTING THIS PROJECT IN YOURSELF" ACTUALLY MEANS

You do not implant SynapseNet. You cannot flash a blockchain into cortex.
That is sci-fi garbage.

What science actually supports is a three-layer stack. The implant (or a
wearable) is a sensor and maybe a stimulator. A local hub you own — phone,
wearable, dedicated box — does the decoding and runs the local model. The
network distributes open software, signed updates, and knowledge. Raw
brain data stays on the hub by default. That is already the design in
IMPLANT_AI_STACK_FOR_SYNAPSENET.txt. I am not changing it.

"Implanting the project" means: your nervous system becomes an input/output
channel to a machine you control, and that machine is a SynapseNet node.
The AI lives next to you, not as a corporation's cloud account inside your
head.


EVIDENCE. NOT FANTASY.

These are real, published, clinical or engineering facts. They are the
bridge from "I like talking to a model" to "a body can talk to a node."


1) THE NON-INVASIVE DOOR (NO SURGERY, START HERE)

   EEG-based brain-computer interfaces have existed for decades. People
   can move a cursor, spell, or trigger commands from scalp voltages.
   The bitrate is low. The signal is noisy. It still works well enough to
   prove the channel exists.

   Evidence:
     Wolpaw JR, Birbaumer N, McFarland DJ, Pfurtscheller G, Vaughan TM.
     "Brain-computer interfaces for communication and control."
     Clinical Neurophysiology. 2002.
     https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12078038/

   Open hardware exists so this does not have to be a closed toy:
     OpenBCI (open-source biosensing).
     https://openbci.com/

   How it maps to SynapseNet:
     EEG / EMG / eye-tracking as Intent v1 sources into the local hub.
     You talk to the local model the same way I started — except the
     "keyboard" can eventually be attention, motor imagery, or a blink
     code. Ugly. Slow. Real.


2) THE PROOF THAT ELECTRONICS CAN LIVE IN THE NERVOUS SYSTEM

   Cochlear implants. Not AI. Not a network. Just the oldest honest
   example: a computer stimulates the auditory nerve and a deaf person
   hears. FDA-cleared for decades. Over a million users worldwide.

   Evidence:
     NIH / NIDCD — Cochlear Implants.
     https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/cochlear-implants

   Deep brain stimulation (DBS) for Parkinson disease is the other
   proof: chronic electrodes in human brain, sometimes with sensing,
   not just blasting current.

   Evidence:
     Limousin P, Krack P, Pollak P, et al. "Electrical stimulation of
     the subthalamic nucleus in advanced Parkinson's disease."
     New England Journal of Medicine. 1998.
     https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199810153391603

     Medtronic Percept — sensing-enabled DBS (local field potentials).
     https://www.medtronic.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/products/neurological/deep-brain-stimulation-systems/percept-pc.html

   Lesson for this project:
     The body can host electronics. That is settled. The unsolved part
     is who owns the software, the data, and the update path.


3) MOTOR INTENT OUT OF CORTEX (THE "I THINK AND THE MACHINE MOVES" PROOF)

   BrainGate and Utah-array work showed that people with tetraplegia can
   control a cursor or a robotic arm from motor-cortex spikes.

   Evidence:
     Hochberg LR, Serruya MD, Friehs GM, et al. "Neuronal ensemble
     control of prosthetic devices by a human with tetraplegia."
     Nature. 2006;442:164-171.
     https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04970

     Hochberg LR, Bacher D, Jarosiewicz B, et al. "Reach and grasp by
     people with tetraplegia using a neurally controlled robotic arm."
     Nature. 2012;485:372-375.
     https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11076

     Collinger JL, Wodlinger B, Downey JE, et al. "High-performance
     neuroprosthetic control by an individual with tetraplegia."
     The Lancet. 2013;381(9866):557-564.
     https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(12)61816-9/fulltext

   Utah array hardware context:
     https://blackrockneurotech.com/products/utah-array/

   How it maps to SynapseNet:
     Decoded intents (move, click, select, confirm) — not raw spikes —
     become the same Intent schema the hub already plans to speak.
     The network never needs the spike tape.


4) SPEECH FROM CORTEX (THE "TALK TO THE MODEL WITHOUT A MOUTH" PROOF)

   This is the one that actually rhymes with why I started. I liked
   talking to a model. Speech neuroprostheses show you can decode
   attempted speech from cortex into text at usable rates.

   Evidence:
     Moses DA, Metzger SL, Liu JR, et al. "Neuroprosthesis for decoding
     speech in a paralyzed person with anarthria."
     New England Journal of Medicine. 2021;385:217-227.
     https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2027540

     Willett FR, Kunz EM, Fan C, et al. "A high-performance speech
     neuroprosthesis."
     Nature. 2023;620:1031-1036.
     https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06377-x

     Metzger SL, Littlejohn KT, Silva AB, et al. "A high-performance
     neuroprosthesis for speech decoding and avatar control."
     Nature. 2023;620:1037-1046.
     https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06443-4

   How it maps to SynapseNet:
     Attempted speech -> local decoder on the hub -> text tokens into
     the local model -> optional knowledge contribution to the network
     if the user opts in. Conversation was the seed. This is conversation
     with the mouth cut out of the loop.


5) LESS HOLE IN THE SKULL: ENDOVASCULAR BCI

   Stentrode-class devices ride a blood vessel instead of opening the
   skull. Still a medical procedure. Still not a weekend project. But
   it is evidence that "get signal from motor cortex" does not have
   to mean a craniotomy.

   Evidence:
     Oxley TJ, Yoo PE, Rind GS, et al. "Motor neuroprosthesis implanted
     with a minimally invasive neurointervention technique."
     Journal of NeuroInterventional Surgery. 2021;13:102-108.
     https://jnis.bmj.com/content/13/2/102

     Synchron — Stentrode platform.
     https://synchron.com/the-stentrode


6) HIGH-CHANNEL THREADS AND SURFACE ARRAYS (THE HARDWARE RACE)

   Thread-based arrays and dense cortical-surface grids are what the
   industry is betting on for bandwidth.

   Evidence:
     Musk E, Neuralink. "An integrated brain-machine interface platform
     with thousands of channels."
     Journal of Medical Internet Research. 2019;21(10):e16194.
     https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e16194/

     Nature Biotechnology review of the BCI landscape (2024):
     https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-024-02195-x

     Precision Neuroscience — 1,024-electrode cortical surface array.
     https://www.precisionneuroscience.com/technology

     Paradromics — high-bandwidth cortical interface.
     https://paradromics.com/connexus-direct-data-interface/

   Neuralink's first-in-human work is an early clinical signal, not a
   consumer product. Treat press releases like press releases.


7) PERIPHERAL NERVES (ARMS AND HANDS, NOT JUST BRAIN)

   You can talk to the nervous system below the skull. Cuff and
   microneedle interfaces on peripheral nerves are a real prosthetic
   path. For a lot of people that is a saner "body interface" than
   cortex.

   Evidence:
     Microneedle cuff electrode arrays, Microsystems & Nanoengineering.
     https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00805-2

   How it maps to SynapseNet:
     Same Intent layer. Different sensor. The kernel should not give a
     shit whether the bits came from motor cortex or a nerve cuff.


HOW YOU ACTUALLY "WEAR" THIS PROJECT (PRACTICAL PATH)

This is the only honest sequence that does not get people killed.

NOW (no implant required)
  • Run a local node. Talk to a local model. That is how this started.
  • Optional: open EEG/EMG (OpenBCI-class) as a research intent source.
  • Keep raw biosignals on the hub. Default deny. Same as the implant spec.

CLINICAL (licensed surgeons, IRB, FDA/CE path — not you in a kitchen)
  • Motor or speech BCI for people who need it: paralysis, anarthria.
  • SynapseNet's job is the open hub protocol, Intent schema, permissions,
    signed updates, rollback. Not the scalpel.

2030-2035 (what I think people will actually need)
  • A cheap standardized implant or wearable that speaks a public HAL.
  • A local hub that runs the model offline.
  • A network that hands out neuro apps and knowledge without a corporation
    owning your brain map.
  • Convenience. Docs. Installers. Safety gates. Free. Open source.

If someone "continues this" after me, that is the part worth continuing.
Not another manifesto. The boring usable layer.


HARD RULES FOR THE BIOHACKING SIDE

I will work on the biohacking part of this project. That means protocols,
HAL, privacy, open drivers, evidence, and making the stack ready.

It does not mean:
  • DIY intracranial electrodes
  • injecting random shit into a brain
  • gene-hacks as a substitute for engineering
  • letting an LLM decide stimulation parameters
  • shipping raw neural data to a network "because mining"

Consensus stays deterministic. Models stay off consensus. The body stays
the user's, not the network's.

If you want to help: make it convenient, make it free, make it so a
stranger can run it. 2030-2035 is not that far. This thing is too big
for one person. That is the whole point of leaving it open.
